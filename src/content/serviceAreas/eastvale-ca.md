## Restoration emergencies common in Eastvale

Eastvale's housing boom happened fast, and a lot of it happened on former dairy and agricultural land with heavy clay soil that doesn't drain quickly. When the Inland Empire gets a wet winter, that clay swells, shifts slab foundations, and pushes water toward homes instead of away from them, which shows up as damp baseboards, buckled flooring, or a musty smell in a garage or laundry room weeks after the storm has passed. Summers run hot and dry, and Santa Ana wind events bring wildfire smoke that settles into HVAC systems and soft furnishings even when the fire itself stayed miles away. Add in dense, newer subdivisions where homes sit close together, and a single irrigation line break, roof leak, or kitchen fire can affect more than one property before anyone notices.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Eastvale

Most of Eastvale was built from the late 1990s through the 2010s, largely on former dairy and agricultural land, which means slab-on-grade foundations, stucco or stone-veneer exteriors, and concrete tile roofs are the norm rather than the exception. That newer construction generally means copper or PEX supply lines instead of older galvanized pipe, which lowers the risk of pipe-related leaks but doesn't eliminate slab leaks or irrigation-line failures.
