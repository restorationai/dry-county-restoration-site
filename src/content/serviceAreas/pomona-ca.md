## Restoration emergencies common in Pomona

Pomona sits in the eastern San Gabriel Valley, where hot, dry summers give way to winter storm systems that can drop a lot of rain in a short window after months of no rain at all. That pattern stresses aging roofs, clogged gutters, and yard drainage that was never designed for a heavy atmospheric river event, and it's a common source of the water intrusion calls we get from the area. Add in the region's wildfire smoke drift during fire season, which can settle into HVAC systems and soft furnishings well away from the actual burn area, and you have the two biggest non-plumbing drivers of restoration work in this part of the valley. Plumbing failures round things out: a mix of older galvanized supply lines and aging drain systems in the city's pre-1960s housing stock means pipe leaks and slab leaks show up on our schedule year-round, not just after storms.

## Services we provide in Pomona

**Water damage restoration** in Pomona often starts behind a wall or under flooring long before a homeowner notices a stain, especially in homes with older copper or galvanized supply lines. We extract standing water, pull moisture readings from framing and subfloor, and set equipment to dry the structure to a verified standard rather than just the surface.

**Fire and smoke damage restoration** covers both structure fires and the smoke and soot migration that follows wildfire activity in the nearby foothills. Soot is acidic and will keep etching into finishes and HVAC components the longer it sits, so containment and cleaning speed matter.

**Mold remediation** usually traces back to a water event that went untreated, whether that's a slow roof leak or a crawlspace that stayed damp after a storm. Mold can colonize exposed wood and drywall within 24 to 48 hours of sustained moisture, which is why we treat drying and mold prevention as the same job.

**Biohazard and trauma cleanup** is handled with the containment and disposal protocols the work requires, for situations property managers and homeowners don't want to face alone.

**Reconstruction** closes out the loss, replacing drywall, flooring, cabinetry, or framing that had to come out during mitigation, so the property is rebuilt, not just dried out.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Pomona

Much of Pomona's older core was built in the early-to-mid 20th century during the citrus and packing-house era, with wood-frame construction, plaster or early drywall interiors, and slab or raised foundations depending on the block. That vintage often carries galvanized steel supply lines and cast iron drain lines nearing or past their service life, along with the possibility of asbestos-containing materials in flooring, texture, or insulation, which changes how a demolition scope has to be handled. Post-war tract development added more slab-on-grade homes with copper plumbing, generally more forgiving but not immune to slab leaks as foundations settle. The San Gabriel Valley's alluvial soil drains reasonably well but can still saturate and shift during heavy, concentrated rain events, putting pressure on foundations and crawlspace vents. Structural repair work in Pomona falls under the City's Building and Safety Division, operating off the state-adopted California Building Code, and most homes here fall within Southern California's active seismic zones, which is a factor in how reconstruction scopes get engineered and permitted.
