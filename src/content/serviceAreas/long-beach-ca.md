Dry County Restoration handles water damage, fire and smoke recovery, mold remediation, sewage cleanup, and reconstruction for homes and businesses throughout Long Beach. Property here ranges from older coastal bungalows to mid-rise commercial buildings near the harbor, and each type of structure fails differently when water, fire, or smoke gets into it. We bring IICRC-trained crews, document everything for insurance, and work the loss until the structure is dry, clean, or rebuilt, whichever the job calls for.

## Restoration emergencies common in Long Beach

Long Beach sits on the coast, which means a marine layer that keeps humidity elevated most of the year, slow evaporation rates, and conditions where a wet wall cavity can stay damp for days instead of drying on its own. Older housing stock in the flatter, inland parts of the city often sits close to grade with limited crawlspace ventilation, so a slab leak or roof intrusion can wick moisture into framing before anyone notices a stain. Heavy winter storm systems, when they arrive, tend to overwhelm aging storm drains and roof systems that were never built for sustained rain, leading to roof leaks, window intrusion, and backed-up area drains. Closer to the port and industrial corridors, commercial buildings deal with their own version of the same problem: flat roofs, HVAC penetrations, and large footprints where a single leak can travel a long way before it is found.

## Services we provide in Long Beach

Water damage restoration and emergency water removal are our most common calls here, usually tied to a failed supply line, a roof leak during a rain event, or a water heater that gives out in a garage or utility closet. Burst pipe cleanup and repair and water leak detection go hand in hand in older homes where galvanized or early copper lines are reaching the end of their service life.

Fire damage restoration and smoke damage restoration cover everything from a contained kitchen fire to smoke and soot that traveled through HVAC ductwork into rooms the fire never reached; odor removal and deodorization is almost always part of that scope. Mold inspection and testing and mold remediation address the growth that shows up after any of the above goes unaddressed in a humid coastal climate, especially in bathrooms, crawlspaces, and behind cabinetry.

Sewage cleanup and sanitization and basement flooding cleanup handle backups and groundwater intrusion. For larger properties, commercial restoration, industrial restoration, and large loss and catastrophic response bring additional crews and equipment to multi-unit buildings and warehouses. Once the structure is dry, reconstruction services, renovations and general contracting, and roofing installation and replacement carry the property from demolition back to a finished space, with contents restoration and storage protecting belongings in the meantime.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Long Beach

Much of Long Beach's housing was built from the 1920s through the 1950s, with wood-frame construction, slab or raised foundations depending on the neighborhood, and plumbing that often started as galvanized steel before later copper repiping. That vintage brings two recurring issues on restoration jobs: galvanized lines that corrode from the inside and fail without warning, and the possibility of asbestos-containing materials in older flooring, siding, or duct insulation, which changes how a demolition scope is planned. Coastal proximity means soil with higher moisture content than inland areas, and foundations and crawlspaces can hold dampness longer after a leak than they would further from the water. Structural repairs and rebuilds require permits through the City of Long Beach Development Services Department, and the city follows California Building Code with seismic design requirements that apply to any significant structural reconstruction.
