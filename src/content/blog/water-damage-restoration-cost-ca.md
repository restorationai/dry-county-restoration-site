## How much does water damage restoration cost in California?

Most California homeowners pay between $3,000 and $8,000 for water damage restoration on a mid-size residential loss. Simple, single-room clean-water events cost less. Losses that spread through multiple rooms, saturate structural materials, or involve contaminated water cost significantly more. The table below covers the most common scenarios.

| Scenario | Typical Cost Range (CA) |
|---|---|
| Single room, Category 1 (clean water, caught early) | $1,500 - $3,500 |
| Single room, Category 2 (gray water, appliance or toilet overflow) | $3,000 - $6,000 |
| Multi-room, Category 1 or 2, hardwood or tile floors | $5,000 - $9,000 |
| Subfloor or wall cavity saturation requiring demo | $6,000 - $12,000 |
| Category 3 (sewage, floodwater, or grossly contaminated water) | $8,000 - $20,000+ |

These are typical industry figures for the Inland Empire and greater Southern California market. Every loss is different, and Dry County Restoration provides a written scope before any work begins so you know exactly what you're authorizing.

## What factors move the cost up or down?

Five variables account for almost all the price variation you'll see between jobs.

**Water category.** The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) classifies water into three categories. Category 1 is clean water from a supply line or appliance inlet, the least expensive to remediate. Category 2 (gray water) includes washing machine discharge, dishwasher overflow, and toilet overflows without solid waste. Category 3 (black water) covers sewage, rising groundwater, and any water that has sat long enough to become grossly contaminated. Cat 3 requires full PPE, antimicrobial treatment, and disposal of porous materials that came into contact with it, which drives costs sharply higher.

**Class of loss (how far the water spread).** The IICRC also defines four classes of water damage based on how much of the affected materials are wet and how deeply. Class 1 is a small, low-porosity area. Class 4 involves specialty drying situations, hardwood floors, concrete, plaster, where standard drying equipment isn't enough. A Class 4 loss in a Corona home with engineered hardwood throughout the main floor is a fundamentally different job than a Class 1 loss in a bathroom.

**Response time.** Water that sits for 24 to 48 hours begins to wick into drywall, insulation, and subfloor materials. After 48 to 72 hours, mold can begin colonizing wet materials, and mold remediation is a separate cost on top of the water damage restoration. Calling for extraction and drying within the first few hours is the single most effective way to keep costs manageable. If you're not sure what to do in the first hours after a loss, the [burst pipe emergency checklist](/blog/burst-pipe-emergency-checklist/) walks through the immediate steps.

**Materials affected.** Ceramic tile and concrete dry faster and cheaper than hardwood, carpet over pad, or drywall with insulation behind it. When water reaches structural framing or subfloor sheathing, selective demolition is often required to allow drying, and that demo adds both labor cost and eventual reconstruction cost.

**Square footage.** More affected area means more equipment, more drying days, and more monitoring visits. A restoration company running three LGR dehumidifiers and six air movers for five days on a 1,200-square-foot affected area has substantially more equipment time than a single-room job.

## What does the water damage restoration process actually involve?

Knowing the phases helps you understand what you're paying for and why cutting corners on any step creates bigger problems downstream.

1. **Emergency contact and dispatch.** A certified technician is dispatched, often within hours. Dry County Restoration operates 24/7, so a midnight pipe failure gets the same response as a weekday call.

2. **Inspection and moisture mapping.** Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map exactly where water has traveled, including inside wall cavities and under flooring that looks dry on the surface. This step defines the scope of the job.

3. **Water extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water first. This is the fastest phase, a good extraction unit can pull hundreds of gallons in an hour. Thorough extraction dramatically shortens drying time.

4. **Selective demolition (when needed).** Saturated drywall, insulation, and flooring that cannot be dried in place is removed. This is not damage, it is the correct technical step that allows structural materials to dry and prevents mold growth inside wall cavities.

5. **Structural drying.** Blue axial air movers and LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers are staged throughout the affected area. The combination creates airflow across wet surfaces and pulls moisture out of the air as it evaporates. Drying typically takes three to five days, with daily moisture readings to confirm progress toward the IICRC S500 drying goals.

6. **Antimicrobial treatment.** EPA-registered antimicrobials are applied to affected surfaces, especially in Category 2 or 3 losses, to inhibit mold and bacterial growth during and after the drying phase.

7. **Monitoring and documentation.** Each visit produces a moisture log, specific readings at specific locations. This documentation is critical for your insurance claim. It proves the drying was performed to standard and gives the adjuster a defensible record.

8. **Clearance and reconstruction.** Once materials reach their dry standard, equipment is removed and the reconstruction phase begins, replacing drywall, flooring, and finishes. Dry County Restoration handles both mitigation and [general contracting](/services/general-contracting/) so you're not managing two separate companies.

## Will homeowners insurance cover water damage restoration in California?

Most standard California homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, a burst pipe, a failed appliance supply line, or an AC unit that overflows. What they typically exclude is gradual damage: a slow leak under a sink that went unnoticed for months, or a roof that's been failing for years.

The practical distinction adjusters use: if you could not have reasonably known about the water before it caused damage, it's usually covered. If the damage shows evidence of long-term moisture intrusion, expect a coverage dispute.

For losses that are covered, your deductible applies first, commonly $1,000 to $2,500 on California homeowners policies. After that, the insurance company pays the restoration costs based on a written scope of work. The documentation your restoration company produces, moisture logs, photos, equipment records, drying reports, is what supports your claim and protects you if the adjuster disputes the scope.

Flood damage from rising groundwater or storm surge is excluded from standard homeowners policies and requires separate [National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance) (NFIP) coverage or a private flood policy.
