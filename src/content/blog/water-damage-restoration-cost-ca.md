## How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in California?

In California, water damage restoration typically costs between $1,500 and $15,000 for residential properties, with most homeowners paying somewhere in the $3,000 to $7,500 range. The wide range reflects how differently losses play out: a half-bathroom with a slow toilet supply leak is a very different job from a second-floor washing machine overflow that soaked through to the subfloor and the ceiling below.

Here's a breakdown by common scenario:

| Scenario | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Small clean-water leak, 1 room, caught within 24 hours | $1,500 - $4,000 |
| Appliance leak (dishwasher, refrigerator, washing machine), 1-2 rooms | $2,500 - $6,000 |
| Bathroom or kitchen supply line failure, drywall and flooring affected | $3,500 - $7,500 |
| Multi-room loss, Category 2 water (gray water) | $5,000 - $10,000 |
| Slab leak with flooring removal and subfloor drying | $4,000 - $9,000 |
| Sewage backup or Category 3 (black water) contamination | $7,000 - $15,000+ |

## What Factors Move the Price Up or Down?

The cost of water damage restoration is not a flat rate. Several factors shift the final number significantly, and understanding them helps you evaluate any estimate you receive.

**Water category.** The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) classifies water losses into three categories. Category 1 (clean water from a supply line or appliance) is the least expensive to remediate. Category 2 (gray water from a dishwasher drain, washing machine, or toilet overflow with no solids) requires more protective measures and disposal protocols. Category 3 (black water, including sewage backups and any water that has sat long enough to grow bacteria) is the most expensive because it triggers full contamination protocols, PPE requirements, and often complete material removal.

**Water class and drying complexity.** Beyond the source, the IICRC also classifies losses by how deeply water has penetrated materials. A Class 1 loss (surface-level, limited area) dries faster and costs less. A Class 3 or 4 loss means water has wicked into walls, subfloors, or structural wood, requiring longer drying cycles, more equipment, and more monitoring visits.

**How long the water sat.** Time is the most underappreciated cost driver. Water that is extracted within 24 to 48 hours can often save drywall and flooring. Water that sat for 72 hours or longer typically requires full material removal because mold colonization begins within that window. If you're reading this after discovering damage that's been there a while, expect higher costs and possibly a mold remediation component as well. See our post on [signs of hidden mold](/blog/signs-of-hidden-mold/) for what to watch for.

**Square footage affected.** Restoration contractors price by the scope of affected area. More square footage means more extraction equipment, more drying equipment staged, more monitoring visits, and more material removal if needed.

**Materials involved.** Hardwood flooring, tile set in mortar beds, and engineered wood all behave differently under water and cost different amounts to dry or replace. Plaster walls, common in older Inland Empire homes, hold moisture differently than paper-faced drywall. Insulation in walls or under floors almost always needs to be removed and replaced.

**Structural components.** If water reached the subfloor, wall framing, or ceiling joists, those materials need to be dried to IICRC standards before reconstruction begins. Structural drying takes longer and requires more equipment.

## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage Restoration in California?

Most standard homeowners policies in California cover sudden and accidental water damage but exclude gradual leaks and flooding. A burst pipe that lets go overnight is typically a covered loss. A slow leak under the sink that went unnoticed for months is typically not, because insurers classify it as a maintenance issue.

Here's how coverage generally breaks down:

- **Covered (typically):** Burst pipes, appliance supply line failures, water heater ruptures, sudden roof leaks from a storm event
- **Not covered (typically):** Gradual leaks, sewer backup (unless you have a separate rider), ground flooding, slab leaks in some policies
- **Flood damage:** Flood insurance is a separate policy, usually through the [National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP)](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance). Standard homeowners policies do not cover rising water from outside the home.

Your deductible applies before coverage kicks in. In California, deductibles on homeowners policies commonly run $1,000 to $2,500, though some policies carry percentage-based deductibles (1-2% of the home's insured value) for certain perils.

Documentation is what protects your claim. A restoration company that provides a detailed written scope, moisture readings with timestamps, and photo documentation of affected areas before and during work gives your adjuster what they need to process the claim cleanly. ## What Does the Water Damage Restoration Process Actually Involve?

Knowing the phases helps you understand what you're paying for and why cutting corners on any step creates bigger problems later.

1. **Emergency contact and assessment.** A certified technician responds, inspects the loss, uses thermal imaging and moisture meters to map the extent of saturation, and classifies the water category and class. This assessment drives the entire scope.

2. **Water extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water. This is the fastest phase but also the most critical: every hour of standing water increases saturation depth and contamination risk.

3. **Material removal (when necessary).** Wet drywall, insulation, baseboards, and flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed. This is not optional when materials are saturated beyond drying thresholds or when Category 2/3 water is involved.

4. **Structural drying.** Blue axial air movers and LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers are staged throughout the affected area. Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days for a standard residential loss, though Class 3 and 4 losses can run longer. Technicians return daily or every other day to take moisture readings and adjust equipment placement.

5. **Monitoring and documentation.** Every moisture reading is logged with a timestamp. This documentation goes to your insurer and confirms that materials were dried to the IICRC S500 standard before reconstruction begins.

6. **Antimicrobial treatment.** Affected surfaces are treated to inhibit mold growth, particularly in Category 2 and 3 losses.

7. **Clearance and reconstruction handoff.** Once moisture readings confirm structural materials are within acceptable ranges, the mitigation phase is complete. Reconstruction (replacing drywall, flooring, cabinetry) is a separate scope. Dry County Restoration handles both mitigation and general contracting, so you're not coordinating two separate companies.

## What Should You Do Right Now?

If the water source is still active, stop it first. Shut off the supply valve at the fixture or the main shutoff for the house. If you're not sure where your main shutoff is, our [burst pipe emergency checklist](/blog/burst-pipe-emergency-checklist/) walks through the steps.

Once the source is controlled, call a certified restoration company before you start pulling up flooring or tearing out drywall yourself. The reason: insurance adjusters need to see documented moisture readings from the affected materials before demolition. If you remove materials before they're documented, you may lose coverage for that portion of the claim.

Do not run fans from the hardware store as a substitute for professional drying equipment. Residential fans move air but don't remove moisture from the air. LGR dehumidifiers do. Running fans without dehumidification can actually spread moisture further into adjacent materials.
