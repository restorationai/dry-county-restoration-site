## Who do you call first after a house fire in Corona?

Call 911 (or confirm your local fire department has already released the scene) and your insurance carrier before you call anyone about repairs. The fire department's all-clear determines when it's safe to re-enter, and most insurers want to know about a loss within 24 to 48 hours.

If Southern California Edison or SoCalGas flagged damaged lines or a gas leak during the response, don't re-enter until that utility clears the property separately. Electrical and gas hazards from a fire often outlast the flames.

Once the structure is released and your claim is open, you're ready for the next call: a company that can secure the property and start documenting the loss for your adjuster. [What to Do After a House Fire in Corona, CA](/blog/what-to-do-after-a-house-fire/) walks through that first-24-hours sequence in more detail.

## When do you call a restoration company instead of a general contractor?

Call a restoration company immediately after the fire department releases the scene, for board-up, tarping, and smoke damage mitigation; save the general contractor conversation for the rebuild phase, after the loss is documented. These are different jobs with different timing.

A broken window, a burned-through roof section, or an open exterior wall needs to be secured the same day the scene is released, both to prevent further loss and because most policies require you to mitigate ongoing damage. That's emergency [board-up and tarping](/services/emergency-board-up-tarping/) work, not a remodel.

Smoke and soot cleanup is its own specialty, too. Soot is acidic and keeps etching into finishes, fabric, and HVAC components the longer it sits, which is why [smoke damage restoration](/services/smoke-damage-restoration/) usually starts before anyone talks about paint colors. California also requires fire-related structural repair work to be done under a licensed contractor; the state's [CSLB consumer guide](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Consumers/Hire_A_Contractor/) explains how to verify a license before you sign anything.

## What happens when you call for fire damage repair?

The first call establishes the scope: what's burned, what's smoke-damaged, what's salvageable, and what your insurer needs to see. Have your policy number, the fire department's incident report number if you have it, and photos of the damage ready before you call.

A crew that responds will typically assess structural stability, board up open exterior points, set up HEPA air scrubbers to control soot and odor, and begin documenting room by room for the claim file. Because the company answers calls around the clock, that assessment doesn't have to wait for a weekday.

The [U.S. Fire Administration's guide on recovering after a home fire](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/after-a-fire/) is a useful independent reference for what to expect in those first days, separate from any company's own process.

## When does the rebuild happen, and who does that part?

Reconstruction starts once mitigation, smoke cleanup, and the insurance scope are settled, and it goes faster when the same company that documented the loss carries it through to framing, drywall, and paint. A clean handoff between phases is where claims tend to stall.

[Fire damage restoration](/services/fire-damage-restoration/) covers the mitigation side: structural drying if water was used to fight the fire, soot removal, odor control. [Reconstruction](/services/reconstruction/) is the build-back: replacing burned framing, drywall, cabinetry, flooring, and finishes to the condition your policy covers. Keeping both phases with one company means the adjuster is working from one continuous file instead of reconciling two contractors' paperwork.
