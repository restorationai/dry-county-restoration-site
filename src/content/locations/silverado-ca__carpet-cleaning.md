Silverado sits up against the Santa Ana Mountains, and that location shapes what ends up in the carpet fibers. Add in the region's wildfire risk, and carpets in homes near open brush can pick up a faint smoke residue even from distant fire events, long before any visible damage shows up.

## Why Silverado Carpets Need a Different Approach

Canyon dust behaves differently than urban grit. It's finer, it's abrasive against carpet fibers, and it tends to settle in layers rather than sitting on the surface, which means a standard once-over with a rental extractor often just redistributes it. Many Silverado properties are also on well water or septic systems common to canyon communities, so we're mindful of water volume and drainage when we're extracting, we don't want runoff pooling near a septic field or overloading a private system built for a single household.

## Our Carpet Cleaning Process

We start with a dry soil removal pass, counter-rotating brushes to lift the packed dust and debris that canyon homes accumulate between cleanings, since skipping this step just turns dry grit into mud once hot water hits it. From there we apply a pre-treatment matched to the stain type, whether that's ash tracking, pet traffic, or general wear, and follow with truck-mounted hot water extraction, cleaned and dried per IICRC S100 carpet cleaning guidelines. For homes further into the canyon where truck-mount hose length becomes a factor, we carry portable extraction units that deliver comparable suction without needing a direct line back to the truck. Stain removal on set-in spots gets individual attention rather than a blanket chemical pass, since over-treating natural fiber carpet common in older canyon homes can cause browning or fiber damage.
