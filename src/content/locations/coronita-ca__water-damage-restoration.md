## Why Coronita Properties See Water Damage Issues

Coronita sits in the Santa Ana River valley just west of Corona, an area where expansive clay soils are common throughout the Inland Empire. That soil swells when wet and contracts when dry, which puts steady stress on slab foundations and the plumbing run through them. Over time that movement is a frequent cause of slow leaks under slab-on-grade homes, the kind that go undetected for weeks because the water is pooling beneath the structure rather than showing up on a visible wall. Add in the area's semi-rural parcels, where well pumps, irrigation lines, and septic systems sit alongside the municipal water supply, and there are more points of failure than a typical dense suburban block. Summer heat also means homeowners run sprinklers and drip irrigation close to foundations, and a cracked line there can mimic a plumbing leak inside the house.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Coronita

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted equipment before it has a chance to wick further into baseboards, subfloor, and insulation. From there we map moisture with meters and thermal imaging, because in a slab home the visible wet spot rarely matches the actual spread underneath. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed and adjusted based on daily moisture readings rather than left running on a fixed schedule. On properties with hardwood or engineered flooring, which show up often in Coronita's custom-built homes, we use targeted drying mats to pull moisture from the wood itself instead of just drying the air around it. We document every reading, which matters later for both insurance and any HOA sign-off.
