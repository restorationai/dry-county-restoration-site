**Dealing with a stubborn odor in your Ontario home or business that air fresheners just mask instead of fix?** Between wildfire smoke drifting in on Santa Ana winds, kitchen fires in older stucco homes near downtown, and the sealed-up, low-airflow construction common in newer Inland Empire tract homes, odors here tend to settle into materials rather than air out on their own. Ontario's dry heat actually bakes smoke and smoke particulate deeper into drywall, insulation, and HVAC ductwork than homeowners expect, which is why surface cleaning rarely solves the problem long term.

## Why Ontario Properties See Persistent Odor Problems

Ontario sits in a part of the Inland Empire that gets hit hard by Santa Ana wind events each fall, pushing wildfire smoke from surrounding foothill fires into homes through attic vents, window gaps, and HVAC returns. That smoke odor bonds to porous surfaces, carpet padding, and fabric far more aggressively in our low-humidity climate than it would in a coastal city, because dry air pulls odor-causing compounds deeper into whatever they touch instead of letting moisture carry them back out. We also see this with cooking grease odor in older homes near the historic downtown core, where original lath-and-plaster walls and aging duct systems trap smells for years. On the commercial side, Ontario's dense concentration of warehouses and distribution space along the I-10 and I-15 corridors brings a different problem: standing water, mildew, or chemical spill odors trapped inside large, poorly ventilated structures that take far longer to clear with open doors and box fans alone.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Ontario

We start by identifying the source material, not just the smell, because masking an odor without removing or sealing the source means it returns within weeks. That often means pulling baseboards, checking behind drywall after a fire or water event, and inspecting HVAC ductwork, since Ontario's central air systems recirculate odor particles through every room of the house if the coils and ducts aren't treated. Depending on the odor type and the materials involved, we use thermal fogging to penetrate the same cracks and fibers that smoke traveled into originally, ozone treatment for unoccupied structures where a stronger oxidizing process is appropriate, or hydroxyl generators when people, pets, or plants need to stay on-site during treatment, which matters a lot in our warmer months when evacuating a household for days isn't realistic. We finish with HVAC and duct treatment so conditioned air isn't just recirculating the problem back through the house.
