## Why Williams Canyon Properties See Smoke Damage Issues

Williams Canyon sits in the kind of steep, brush-lined terrain that funnels wildfire smoke straight toward rooflines instead of letting it disperse. Afternoon downslope winds common to canyon geography push particulate back into attic vents and soffits even on days when a fire is burning miles away, which is why homeowners here often call us about smoke odor without ever having flames near the property. Older canyon homes with wood siding and single-pane windows are especially porous. Smoke works into the grain of exposed wood framing and settles on HVAC coils, and once that soot cools it oxidizes and bonds to porous surfaces within a matter of days. Waiting even a week to address it usually means replacing materials that could have been cleaned.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Williams Canyon

We start by walking the property to separate wet smoke residue (thick, sticky, usually from a slow-burning structure fire) from dry wildfire soot, because the cleaning agents and pressure settings are different for each. Hard surfaces get dry sponging or chemical sponging depending on residue type, HVAC systems get isolated and cleaned separately so soot doesn't recirculate through the ductwork, and porous materials like upholstery or drapery are evaluated item by item rather than written off automatically. For canyon properties with exposed beam ceilings or knotty pine interiors, we test clean a hidden section first since raw wood reacts unpredictably to standard deodorizing agents. Thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation follows once surfaces are clean, targeting the odor molecules that have worked into wall cavities rather than just masking them.
