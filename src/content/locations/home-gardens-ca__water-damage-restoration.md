## Why Home Gardens Properties See Water Damage Issues

Home Gardens is unincorporated, which means permitting and inspections for repair work run through Riverside County rather than a city building department, something we account for when we scope a job that needs more than extraction and drying. The housing stock here is a mix: older slab-on-grade ranch homes from the mid-century boom alongside manufactured and mobile homes, both of which handle water intrusion very differently than newer stick-built construction. Flat lot grading across the area slows surface drainage after the heavy winter rain events that roll through inland Riverside County, and that runoff has nowhere to go but toward foundations and crawlspace vents. Combine that with the summer heat stress that cracks galvanized supply lines and water heater fittings, and you get two very different seasons for two very different kinds of water loss.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Home Gardens

We start with moisture mapping, because a slab home and a manufactured home hide water in different places. On slab foundations we check for wicking up through drywall and baseboards; in manufactured housing we're more concerned with the belly wrap and insulation beneath the floor joists, which can trap water for weeks if it's not caught early. Extraction comes next, pulling free water with truck-mount or portable units depending on access, followed by containment and structural drying set up to the IICRC S500 standard. We track moisture content daily with meters rather than guessing based on how a surface feels, and we don't pull equipment until the readings confirm the structure, not just the surface, is dry.

## Local note

Manufactured and mobile homes make up a meaningful share of Home Gardens' housing stock, and their underbelly construction, vapor barrier, insulation, and steel chassis, holds moisture differently than a conventional slab home. A water heater failure that would dry out in three or four days under a typical house can take noticeably longer under a manufactured home because airflow beneath the structure is limited. We adjust our drying equipment placement and timeline for these homes instead of applying a one-size approach that works fine on a stick-built ranch house but leaves hidden moisture behind in a mobile home's floor assembly.
