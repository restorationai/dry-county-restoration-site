**Renovations, remodels and general contracting in Long Beach** often start with a different question than they would in a newer suburb: is this project a straightforward upgrade, or does it need to work around a 1920s foundation, old cast iron plumbing, or damage left behind by a prior leak. Dry County Restoration handles both scenarios, planning the build around what's actually behind the walls rather than assuming a clean slate.

## Why Long Beach Properties Need a Different Approach

Long Beach's housing stock spans a wide range of eras, from Craftsman and Spanish Revival bungalows built before WWII to tract homes from the 1950s and 60s postwar boom, plus newer multifamily and infill construction closer to downtown. Older homes frequently have knob-and-tube wiring remnants, galvanized or cast iron supply lines, and single-wall plaster construction that behaves differently than drywall once you open it up. The coastal marine layer also keeps humidity elevated for much of the year, which matters when we're sequencing framing, drying time, and finish work on a remodel, especially in homes closer to the shoreline where salt air accelerates corrosion on older metal fixtures and fasteners.

## Our Renovations, Remodels and General Contracting Process

We start with a walkthrough that looks past the finishes to the structure: plumbing age and material, electrical panel capacity, signs of prior water intrusion, and whether the home's foundation type (raised pier-and-beam is common in Long Beach's older neighborhoods) will affect how we frame a kitchen or bathroom layout. From there we scope the work, pull the necessary permits through the city, and sequence demolition, rough-in, and finish stages so the homeowner has a realistic timeline instead of a guess. For post-damage rebuilds, this is also where we coordinate directly with the carrier's adjuster on scope and materials before work begins, so the rebuild isn't held up waiting on paperwork once drywall is open.

## Kitchen, Bathroom, and Post-Damage Rebuild Work

Kitchen and bathroom remodels in Long Beach's older housing stock usually involve more than a cosmetic refresh. Cast iron drain lines under a 1930s bungalow often need evaluation before a new tile floor goes down, and galvanized supply lines feeding an original bathroom are a common reason a "simple" remodel turns into a repipe. When the project follows water, fire, or storm damage, we treat the rebuild as a continuation of the restoration rather than a separate job: the same team that documented the loss and handled drying or demolition carries that knowledge into framing, electrical, plumbing, and finish work, which keeps the scope accurate and avoids re-opening walls that were already inspected.

## Permitting and Coordination in Long Beach

General contracting work in Long Beach runs through the city's permitting process, and projects that touch structural elements, electrical panels, or plumbing systems in older homes typically require inspection sign-off at multiple stages, not just at final. For homes in multifamily buildings or planned developments with an HOA, we also factor in association approval timelines before scheduling exterior work or anything that affects shared systems. Building this into the schedule upfront, rather than discovering it mid-project, is part of what keeps a remodel or rebuild on track.

## Local note

One detail that catches homeowners off guard during a kitchen or bath remodel in Long Beach's older single-family neighborhoods: plaster-and-lath walls common in pre-1950s construction don't always match current electrical code when you open them up, even if the house passed inspection decades ago. We flag this during the walkthrough rather than after demo starts, so the permit scope and budget account for any required upgrades instead of surprising the homeowner partway through the job.
