## Why Williams Canyon Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of the housing stock built into or near the canyon slopes sits on a mix of slab-on-grade and raised foundations, often on the same structure where the lot steps down the hillside. That split foundation style means water from a failed supply line or a slow roof leak can travel under the house on the downhill side for days before it ever shows up as a stain or a soft spot on the uphill rooms. Southern California's swing from extended dry stretches to sudden, heavy rain events also stresses slab foundations and irrigation lines that were never built for saturated soil, and hillside grading means runoff doesn't always go where the original drainage plan intended. Add in older washing machine hoses, water heaters nearing the end of their service life, and irrigation systems tied close to the foundation, and you get a steady stream of water losses that start small and get expensive fast if they sit.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Williams Canyon

We start with moisture mapping, not guesswork: thermal imaging and penetrating moisture meters to find where water actually traveled, not just where it's visible. We monitor daily with the same meters used at intake so drying times are based on actual readings, not a fixed calendar. For homes where water has moved under flooring or behind baseboards on a split-level foundation, we track both sides of the structure separately since they rarely dry at the same rate.
